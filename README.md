## Evaluation Criteria

Score each vendor from 1 to 5 on every row, multiply by the weight, and total the results.

| Criterion | What to verify | Weight/Why |
|---|---|---|
| Operating history | Years in business and whether the leadership has run online businesses, not only client accounts | High: long tenure across algorithm changes signals durable methods |
| Owned-asset testing | Whether the agency builds and manages its own websites or digital properties | High: strategies tested on owned assets carry less guesswork onto your site |
| Leadership involvement | Who sets strategy and whether the founder or a senior strategist stays engaged | Medium: founder-led shops often keep strategy closer to the decision-maker |
| Industry range | Breadth of verticals served and how methods adapt across them | Medium: cross-industry exposure surfaces tactics a single-niche shop may miss |
| Service depth | Whether SEO connects to PR, CRO, affiliate and e-commerce work | Medium: rankings without conversion or authority work stall out |
| Local presence | Physical office and understanding of the Fort Lauderdale and Broward County market | Medium: local context matters for map pack and service-area queries |
| Reporting clarity | What metrics are reported, how often, and in what format | High: you need to see progress tied to leads and revenue |
| Contract clarity | Scope, deliverables, and exit terms in writing | High: ambiguity here is the most common source of disputes |

## Local SEO Checklist for Fort Lauderdale

Any Fort Lauderdale SEO engagement should cover these local fundamentals. Use this list to confirm the vendor's plan addresses each item.

- [ ] Google Business Profile claimed, verified, and fully completed with categories, hours, and services
- [ ] Name, address, and phone number consistent across the website and major directories
- [ ] Dedicated service pages for Fort Lauderdale and relevant Broward County neighborhoods or nearby cities
- [ ] Location-specific content that reflects real local context, not swapped city names
- [ ] Local schema markup (LocalBusiness or a relevant subtype) implemented and validated
- [ ] Review generation process in place, with a plan for responding to reviews
- [ ] Local link opportunities identified, such as chambers, associations, and South Florida media
- [ ] Call tracking and form tracking set up so local leads are measurable
- [ ] Map pack and organic rankings tracked separately for priority local queries

## Red Flags

| Signal | Why it's risky |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed first-page or number-one rankings | No one controls Google's algorithm; guarantees suggest shortcuts or misleading claims |
| Vague deliverables | You cannot hold a vendor accountable for work that was never defined |
| No access to your own accounts | You should own your analytics, Search Console, and Business Profile access |
| Reporting focused only on rankings | Rankings without traffic, leads, or revenue data hide whether the work pays off |
| Bulk link packages | Low-quality links can trigger penalties and take months to clean up |
| No discussion of conversion | Traffic that does not convert wastes budget |
| Long lock-in with no exit terms | Limits your options if performance or communication falls short |

## Interview Questions

Ask every shortlisted vendor the same questions and record answers verbatim for comparison.

1. How long has your leadership team worked in search, and have they built or run online businesses of their own?
2. Do you test strategies on properties you own before applying them to client sites?
3. Who will set strategy for my account, and how often will that person be involved?
4. What does the first 90 days look like, and which deliverables will I see?
5. Which metrics do you report, and how do they connect to leads or revenue?
6. How do you approach link building and digital PR, and what do you avoid?
7. How do SEO and conversion rate optimization work together in your process?
8. What local factors matter most for ranking in Fort Lauderdale and Broward County?
9. Will I retain ownership of all accounts, content, and data if we part ways?
10. What are the contract terms, and what are the conditions for ending the engagement?

## FAQ

**How do I choose an SEO company in Fort Lauderdale?**
Score each vendor against the evaluation criteria table, confirm service coverage matches your goals, check for red flags, and ask the same interview questions of every candidate. Favor vendors with verifiable operating history and clear reporting.

**What is the difference between Fort Lauderdale SEO and general SEO?**
Fort Lauderdale SEO adds local factors on top of standard practice: Google Business Profile optimization, map pack visibility, local citations, reviews, and content that reflects the South Florida market.

**Should I hire an SEO specialist or a full digital marketing agency?**
If organic rankings are your only gap, a specialist may be enough. If you also need authority building, conversion improvements, affiliate growth, or marketplace visibility, a digital marketing agency with broader internet marketing coverage avoids coordinating several vendors.

**How long before SEO shows results?**
It depends on competition and site condition. Get milestone expectations in writing, not ranking promises.
